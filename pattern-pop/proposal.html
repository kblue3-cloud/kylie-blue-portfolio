01

Introduce the game

Experimental visual puzzle game

PATTERNPOP●

Every pattern has a story.

Pattern Pop is a short, colorful browser puzzle game where players explore a strange geometric world and discover hidden visual patterns. At first, the world looks random, but players gradually learn its rules by observing shapes, colors, sequences, and repetitions. By finding and activating the correct patterns, players transform the environment and bring the world into harmony.

Research draft

Play-session evidence plan

Draft text · verify before submission
My gameplay observations

Draft to verify during play: Little Alchemy 2 appears to support experimentation by making combinations quick to try and visually easy to read. A successful combination introduces a new element, while an unsuccessful attempt allows the player to continue without a harsh penalty. During my own play session, I will verify the exact animations, interface messages, and sequence of actions that create this learning loop.

Three specific moments
  1. First discovery — document the first successful combination, its visual response, and how it affects the next choice.
  2. Unsuccessful experiment — record one combination that produces no new result and how the interface encourages another attempt.
  3. Transferred knowledge — identify a moment when an earlier discovery suggests a later combination.
What I learned

Draft conclusion: the most useful design principle is that experimentation feels inviting when actions are immediate, feedback is legible, and unsuccessful attempts do not erase progress. Pattern Pop applies this idea through shape selection: every click produces a visible response, hints support another attempt, and successful choices gradually restore the same geometric world.

03

Critical discussion with AI

AI as a design partner, not evidence

Question. Challenge.
Revise. Decide.

This section separates AI suggestions from decisions I still need to verify. The value of the process is not agreement; it is using questions to make the game more specific and buildable.

A

Initial idea + suggestions

The original concept focused on matching repeating geometric patterns. AI suggested organizing the experience into three stages, using click-based interaction, and giving immediate visual feedback. This helped turn a broad idea into a short sequence with a beginning, development, and ending.

B

What I verified / will verify

  • A three-stage structure is achievable for a small browser prototype.
  • Visual feedback can help communicate puzzle rules.
  • Predetermined solutions make each puzzle testable.

These are design decisions and implementation checks—not independent research findings.

C

Gaps + limitations

  1. The concept could become too predictable and feel like an ordinary matching game.
  2. Environmental change did not yet carry enough narrative meaning.
  3. The technical scope for changing patterns needed clearer boundaries.
D

Revision principle

Keep the solutions fixed and understandable, but let motion, color, and rearrangement alter the player's first impression. The final prototype should make each correct choice visibly repair part of the same world.

Completed draft exampleVerify against your actual AI conversation and add screenshots if required.
01 / My original concern

My original concern was that a simple pattern-matching game might be too predictable. I wanted the world to feel surprising while keeping the puzzles understandable.

02 / AI suggestion

Keep the puzzle rules consistent but use movement, color changes, and rearrangements to encourage players to reconsider their first impressions.

03 / My challenge or follow-up

How can movement and color make the world feel surprising without making the actual pattern rules confusing? I also need the idea to remain realistic for a short HTML, CSS, and JavaScript prototype with only three stages.

04 / Revised suggestion

Use one clear rule per stage and keep every solution predetermined. Add surprise through the environment’s response rather than through ambiguous answers: restore color after Stage 1, transform the outlier after Stage 2, and connect the full composition after Stage 3. Reuse the same click interaction and visual components so the scope stays manageable.

05 / My conclusion

I will keep the solutions predetermined while using animation and visual changes to make discovery feel more experimental.

06 / Design decision

Each completed stage restores one layer of the world: rhythm, consistency, then connection. Animation communicates that the player is changing a shared environment rather than answering unrelated questions.

04

Narrative + concept

A world that teaches through change

From scattered
to connected.

Pattern Pop takes place in a colorful world made entirely from geometric forms. At first, its objects appear scattered and unrelated. The player discovers that the world is not random: it follows a visual language built from repetition, difference, and connection.

The player is an observer and experimenter—not a named hero. Each click is a hypothesis. Each response teaches one small part of the rule. As the three stages are solved, still shapes begin to move, isolated colors return, and separate pieces join into one composition.

Story is expressed through the environment becoming legible—not through dialogue or exposition.

B / Shape

Familiar forms,
unfamiliar behavior.

Circles, triangles, squares, and soft irregular forms are easy to name. Rotation, bounce, overlap, and scale give them personality.

C / Interface

✓ Pattern found× Look once more

Thick outlines, large targets, icons, and text ensure that feedback never depends on color alone.

D / Motion

AnticipateRespondSettle

Motion should explain relationships: a quick selection pop, a gentle wrong-answer wobble, and a larger coordinated stage resolution. Reduced-motion preferences remove nonessential animation.

Optional sound palette

Small sounds,
clear meaning.

Generated sound supports feedback without becoming required. Every sound event has a matching visual state. Background music remains optional and outside the minimum prototype scope.

Borrowing / changing

The proposal borrows the principle of low-risk experimentation from Little Alchemy 2. Pattern Pop changes the subject, interaction, visual system, and narrative response: the player selects relationships in a geometric composition, and each answer visibly restores one shared world. 07

Two-week build plan

Realistic scope, testable outcome

Build. Observe.
Refine.

The plan prioritizes a complete, short experience over adding more features. The final result should work from opening to ending before visual polish expands.

Week 2

Build the prototype

One complete interaction loop

  1. 01Set up the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript structure.
  2. 02Create the opening screen and responsive layout.
  3. 03Implement click/tap selection and shared puzzle data.
  4. 04Build all three deterministic puzzle stages.
  5. 05Add feedback, transitions, simple animation, and optional sound.
  6. 06Create the ending and verify the complete path.
Deliverable

A start-to-finish browser prototype with three solvable stages.

Week 3

Test and refine

Clarity before complexity

  1. 01Observe a small number of players using the game.
  2. 02Record where rules or feedback are confusing.
  3. 03Adjust puzzle difficulty, hints, and response timing.
  4. 04Test desktop and mobile layouts.
  5. 05Check keyboard access and reduced motion.
  6. 06Refine typography, color, sound, animation, and bugs.
Deliverable

A tested, documented, and publishable final build.

Scope boundary

No 3D graphics, procedural generation, accounts, leaderboards, timers, or dependencies are required. Three authored puzzles, one consistent input, and a polished response system are enough.
